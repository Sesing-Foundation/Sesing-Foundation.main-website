The Extended First-Year Mentorship Architecture

Why Stop Mentorship at the University Gate?

South African universities invest heavily in First-Year Experience and Mentorship Programmes to support new students once they arrive on campus. Sesing Foundation NPC bridges the gap between high school and higher education by extending those trained university mentors directly to high school learners before matric ends.

Step 01 • Campus Network

1. University First-Year Mentor Network

We partner with senior undergraduate and postgraduate students who already serve as trained mentors within existing university First-Year Mentorship Programmes across South African institutions.

  • • Pre-Trained Peer Leaders: Mentors already possess university orientation training, faculty insight, and peer support skills.
  • • Alumni Giving Back: University students from townships and rural towns return to mentor learners in their home provinces.
  • • Real Academic Experience: Mentors share honest, practical insights on university workloads, laboratory sessions, and campus life.
Source: Existing Campus Programmes Step 1 of 3 →
Step 02 • High School Bridge

2. High School Pre-Transition Outreach

Instead of waiting for learners to reach university, we extend these trained university mentors into Grade 11 and Grade 12 classrooms at high schools such as Moroka Senior Secondary School, alongside foundational academic aspiration outreach at Tawana Primary School in Thaba Nchu.

  • • APS & Degree Matching: Helping learners calculate their Admission Point Score (APS) and select realistic degree and diploma options.
  • • University & NSFAS Applications: Guided support for online university applications, residence booking, and financial aid readiness.
  • • Academic Literacy & Exam Discipline: Equipping Grade 11 and 12 learners with university-calibre study techniques and past-paper strategies.
Focus: Moroka Senior Secondary & Thaba Nchu Step 2 of 3 →
Step 03 • First-Year Success

3. Seamless First-Year Campus Landing

When matriculants pass and arrive on campus for their first year, they do not arrive as strangers. They transition directly into the university's existing First-Year Mentorship Programme already connected to peer mentors in their faculty.

  • • Eliminating First-Year Shock: Learners already understand lecture pacing, learning management systems, and faculty expectations.
  • • Warm Handover on Campus: Pre-existing relationships with first-year mentors reduce isolation and early academic dropout.
  • • The Mentorship Cycle Continues: Successful first-year students go on to become campus mentors who return to mentor their high schools.
Outcome: Higher First-Year Retention Complete Continuum

Stage 01 • Grade 11/12 Academic Alignment

Aligning Grade 11 & 12 Subject Performance With University Admission Benchmarks

Many high school learners only realise in Grade 12 that South African universities use final Grade 11 marks to grant initial conditional offers. Our extended university mentors engage Grade 11 and Grade 12 learners early so they understand how subject percentages translate into Admission Point Scores (APS).

What University Mentors Cover in This Stage:
  • • Understanding how Grade 11 final promotion results determine conditional university acceptance in early Grade 12.
  • • Mapping subject combinations (such as Mathematics, Physical Sciences, Life Sciences, Geography, and Accounting) to specific university faculties.
  • • Introducing university-style active recall, past-paper analysis, and time management habits before matric finals begin.
Mentor Session Outcome

Every participating learner completes a Personal University Target Sheet listing three realistic degree or diploma pathways, the exact APS required for each, and a subject improvement plan.

Statutory Data Protection Notice

POPIA Section 18 Learner & Mentor Privacy Notice

Responsible Party: Sesing Foundation NPC (CIPC Reg. No. 2026/535520/08)
Operational Base: Thaba Nchu, Free State, Republic of South Africa
Governing Statute: Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA)

1. Purpose of Information Processing (POPIA Section 18)

Sesing Foundation NPC collects educator, school, university mentor, and learner workshop inquiry details strictly for coordinating high school-to-university mentorship sessions, faculty matching, and academic transition guidance.

2. Safeguarding High School Learners (POPIA Sections 34 & 35)

All school mentorship workshops are conducted in partnership with school leadership and educators. Where learners are under the age of 18, participation in ongoing individual mentorship requires school and parent/guardian consent in full compliance with Sections 34 and 35 of POPIA. The NSC APS Calculator on this website runs entirely inside your browser and does not store or transmit learner marks.

3. University Mentor Vetting & Code of Conduct

University student mentors participating in high school outreach must adhere to the Sesing Foundation NPC Learner Safeguarding Code of Conduct and POPIA confidentiality standards.
